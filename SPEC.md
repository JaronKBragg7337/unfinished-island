# Experiment contract

Build a persistent world with an operational computer whose resident authors a child simulation. Keep the resulting program, run it, inspect it, enter it, and retain its ancestry. Future growth includes manufacturing, exchange between worlds, and richer resident tools.

First experiment: resident Mara, powered by local Qwen, receives an explicit assignment to create a small foraging world. It writes a declarative Island Language program with ordered behavior rules, a resource layout, and a hypothesis. The same interpreter runs in Node and the browser. No claim of consciousness, spontaneous computer discovery, unconstrained coding, or unlimited recursion.

World time advances by persisted simulation steps on the MSI, independent of viewers. The browser interpolates/replays snapshots and clearly labels its local child-world execution. A trusted supervisor serializes cycles and publication. Failed generations are retained as failures. Historical programs are immutable. Updates never manufacture successful experiments or fake model activity.

Visual units are metres. Assets have stable IDs and assembled parts. Material provenance accompanies copied CC0 assets. Parent island is an original field station; its station dimensions are design decisions, not certified engineering. Child simulations initially use a simplified terrain and resource model.

Phone controls: left touch movement stick appears only while pressed; right drag look in walk mode. Orbit and walking supported. Inspection includes IDs, metre coordinates and collision bounds. Phone performance is measured, not assumed. Desktop browser mobile emulation is not physical iPhone verification.

Policy revisions added 2026-09-06: environmental fields are fixed to the selected predecessor. A revision records `revision.of`, paired evidence, adoption decision and reason. `policyChampionId` records which policy the next assignment builds on; rejected and tied proposals remain in history. This is assigned rule optimization, not spontaneous self-improvement.
